Key Vocabulary

Note: A single sound. There are 12 different notes.

Sharp (#): One fret higher. C# is one fret higher than C.

Flat (♭): One fret lower. Db is one fret lower than D. (Same as C#!)

Octave: The distance from one note to the same note higher. 12 notes = 1 octave.

Semitone/Half Step: The distance between any two notes right next to each other. = 1 fret on guitar.

Tone/Whole Step: The distance of 2 semitones. = 2 frets on guitar.

Interval: The distance between two notes. Can be measured in semitones.

Scale: A collection of notes in a specific order. The Major scale and Minor scale are the most common.

Chord: Three or more notes played together. Major chords sound happy, minor chords sound sad.

Tab/Tablature: A way of writing music for guitar. Shows you what fret to play on what string.

The Major Scale Formula

Every major scale follows this pattern (in whole steps and half steps):

W - W - H - W - W - W - H

(W = Whole Step = 2 frets, H = Half Step = 1 fret)

The Minor Scale Formula (Natural Minor)

Every minor scale follows this pattern:

W - H - W - W - H - W - W

Major Chord Formula

To build a major chord: Root + Major 3rd + Perfect 5th

In semitones: Start note + 4 semitones + 3 semitones

Example: C Major

  • C (root)
  • E (4 semitones up from C)
  • G (3 semitones up from E)

Minor Chord Formula

To build a minor chord: Root + Minor 3rd + Perfect 5th

In semitones: Start note + 3 semitones + 4 semitones

Example: A Minor

  • A (root)
  • C (3 semitones up from A)
  • E (4 semitones up from C)

Common Chord Progressions

I-IV-V (The Most Common)

This progression is in thousands of songs. It sounds "complete" and happy.

I-V-vi-IV

Very popular in modern pop and rock. Sounds catchy and emotional.

vi-IV-I-V (Sadder variation)

The same chords as I-V-vi-IV but starting on vi, which makes it feel more emotional.

Roman Numeral Notation

Chords in a key are represented by Roman numerals. In a major key:

  • I (1): Major chord
  • ii (2): Minor chord
  • iii (3): Minor chord
  • IV (4): Major chord
  • V (5): Major chord (often dominant 7th)
  • vi (6): Minor chord
  • vii° (7): Diminished chord (rarely used)
