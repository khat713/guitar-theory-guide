MODULE 5
CHORD THEORY

A chord is just 3 notes played at the same time. Learn one simple recipe and you can build any chord on the whole guitar!

What Is a Chord?

A chord is when you play 3 or more notes at the same time. Strum a few strings together — boom — that's a chord!

The simplest chord is called a triad, which just means "3 notes." Every beginner chord you know (Em, G, C, Am, D) is a triad.

💡
Chords aren't magic hand shapes — they follow a simple recipe. Learn the recipe and you can make any chord, anywhere on the neck!

Chords Are Stacked Notes

Every chord starts on a root note (the note the chord is named after) and adds two more notes on top. Think of it like stacking building blocks — always the same recipe.

Triad = Root  +  interval_1  +  interval_2 The two intervals decide if the chord sounds happy, sad, or spooky
Root The starting note. C chord → C is the root. This is home base.
Third The 2nd note of the chord. It decides if the chord sounds happy or sad.
Fifth The 3rd note of the chord. It gives the chord its full, solid sound.

The Major Chord Formula

Major chords sound happy and bright — like sunshine! The recipe is super simple: count frets up from your root note.

Major chord = Root  +  4 semitones  +  3 semitones Root → up 4 frets → up 3 more frets. That's it!

A semitone is just one fret on your guitar. Go up 4 frets for the second note, then 3 more frets for the third.

Building Four Essential Chords

The Minor Chord Formula

Minor chords sound sad or mysterious — like a rainy day. The recipe is almost the same as major, but the first step is 3 frets instead of 4.

Minor chord = Root  +  3 semitones  +  4 semitones Root → up 3 frets → up 4 more frets. Just swap the numbers!

That one fret is the whole secret. Move just one fret and you flip the mood from happy to sad — that's the power of music!

Comparing Major and Minor

The only difference between major and minor is one single fret. That tiny change flips the whole mood of the chord!

Major chord Root + 4 frets + 3 frets. Sounds happy, bright, and strong.
Minor chord Root + 3 frets + 4 frets. Sounds sad, dark, and moody.
⚠
The big secret: major vs. minor is just one fret difference on the middle note. One fret — two completely different feelings. How cool is that?!

Other Chord Types

Major and minor are the most common, but there are two more types worth knowing. Each one has its own special feeling!

Major Root + 4 + 3. Happy and bright. Used in tons of pop and folk songs.
Minor Root + 3 + 4. Sad and moody. Used in blues, rock, and ballads.
Diminished Root + 3 + 3. Tense and spooky — like something weird is about to happen.
Augmented Root + 4 + 4. Dreamy and floating — like you're waiting for something.

The Diminished Chord

The diminished chord stacks two groups of 3 frets. It sounds spooky — perfect for horror movie music!

Diminished chord = Root  +  3 semitones  +  3 semitones Root → up 3 frets → up 3 more frets

Example: B diminished. Start on B. Add 3 frets → D. Add 3 more → F. Notes: B, D, F.

💡
Diminished chords sound creepy on their own, but in songs they create exciting tension. You'll hear them in jazz and dramatic rock!

Your Chord Shapes Already Use These Formulas

Every chord shape you learned as a beginner is just one of these recipes applied to a starting note. You were already doing music theory without knowing it!

✓
Pick any chord you already know. Find the root note, count up to the next notes, and see if it matches the major or minor formula. It will — every time!

Every Chord Has a Formula

Here's the best part: you only need 4 short recipes to build any chord ever. No memorizing 40 different shapes — just 4 formulas you can use anywhere!

Major: R + 4 + 3    Minor: R + 3 + 4    Dim: R + 3 + 3    Aug: R + 4 + 4 All intervals in semitones (frets). Apply to any root note to get that chord's three notes

Practice

Exercise 1: Build a D Major Chord

Use the major recipe (Root + 4 frets + 3 frets) to build D major. Count up from D: D – D# – E – F – F# – G – G# – A.

Major formula: Root + 4 semitones + 3 semitones
Root: D

Step 1: Count 4 frets up from D.
Step 2: Count 3 more frets up from that note.
What are the three notes of D major?
D major, step by step:
  • Root: D
  • +4 frets: D → D# → E → F → F#
  • +3 frets: F# → G → G# → A
D major = D, F#, A

When you play an open D chord, the strings make D, A, D, and F# — exactly those three notes!

Exercise 2: Build a B Minor Chord

Use the minor recipe (Root + 3 frets + 4 frets) to build B minor. Count up from B: B – C – C# – D – D# – E – F – F#.

Minor formula: Root + 3 semitones + 4 semitones
Root: B

Step 1: Count 3 frets up from B.
Step 2: Count 4 more frets up from that note.
What are the three notes of B minor?
B minor, step by step:
  • Root: B
  • +3 frets: B → C → C# → D
  • +4 frets: D → D# → E → F → F#
B minor = B, D, F#

These notes all fit in the G major scale — that's why Bm sounds great with G, D, and C chords!
