How to Practice

Practice is where theory becomes real. You can read all the lessons you want, but understanding only happens when you practice. Here's how to use these exercises:

  1. Try each question without looking at the answer
  2. Write down your answer (or at least think it through)
  3. Check the answer — if you got it right, great! If not, look at the explanation
  4. Do the next question
💡 Pro Tip: If you get something wrong, don't just read the answer. Go back to the lesson and understand WHY the answer is right. That's where learning happens.

Module 1 Practice: The 12 Notes

Q1: What are the 12 notes of music? Put them in order starting from C.

Answer: C - C# - D - D# - E - F - F# - G - G# - A - A# - B

Explanation: These 12 notes make up the chromatic scale. They're the only notes in Western music, and they repeat up and down in the same order. Memorizing this order is one of the most important things you'll do.

Q2: What does the # (sharp) symbol mean?

Answer: Sharp means "one fret higher" or "one semitone higher."

Explanation: When you see C#, it means the note one fret higher than C. On your guitar, if C is on fret 8, then C# is on fret 9. The # symbol always means "raise it by one fret."

Q3: How many notes are between C and C (going up)? Counting C at the start but not C at the end.

Answer: 12 notes

Explanation: C - C# - D - D# - E - F - F# - G - G# - A - A# - B = 12 notes. This 12-note pattern is called an octave.

Q4: On the low E string, E is the open string (fret 0). What fret is F?

Answer: Fret 1

Explanation: F is one fret up from E. That's why it's at fret 1. Remember: each fret = one note higher.

Q5: What note is on fret 3 of the low E string?

Answer: G

Explanation: Fret 0 = E, Fret 1 = F, Fret 2 = F#, Fret 3 = G. Count up from E: E (0) → F (1) → F# (2) → G (3).

Module 2 Preview Practice: The Fretboard

Q6: The same 12-note pattern appears on every string. True or False?

Answer: True

Explanation: This is one of the most important ideas in guitar theory. The same pattern of 12 notes repeats on all 6 strings. The only difference is that each string starts on a different note.
