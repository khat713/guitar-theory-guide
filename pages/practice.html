Module 1: The 12 Notes

Q1: What are the 12 notes of music? Write them in order, starting from C.

Answer

C, C#, D, D#, E, F, F#, G, G#, A, A#, B

These 12 notes are the building blocks of all music. Memorize this list; it's the most important thing in the whole course!

Q2: What does the # (sharp) symbol mean in practical terms on a guitar?

Answer

One fret higher. C# is one fret above C on any string.

Q3: How many notes are between one C and the next C, counting the first C but not the last?

Answer: 12 notes

C C# D D# E F F# G G# A A# B = 12 notes. This 12-note span is one octave.

Q4: On the low E string, E is the open string (fret 0). What fret is F?

Answer: Fret 1

F is one semitone above E, which is one fret. E (0) → F (1).

Q5: What note is on fret 3 of the low E string?

Answer: G

Count up from E: E(0) → F(1) → F#(2) → G(3).

Q6: True or false: the 12-note pattern repeats on every string of the guitar.

Answer: True

Same pattern on all 6 strings. Only the starting note (open string) differs.

Q7: Are C# and D♭ the same note or different notes?

Answer: Same note

They are enharmonic equivalents: two different names for the same physical pitch and fret.
